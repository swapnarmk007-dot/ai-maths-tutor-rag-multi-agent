Calculus & Integration Techniques Reference Guide
Chapter 1: The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus establishes the vital link between differentiation and integration.
Part 1 states that if f is continuous on [a, b], then the function g(x) = ∫_{a}^{x} f(t) dt has derivative g'(x) = f(x).
Part 2 states that ∫_{a}^{b} f(x) dx = F(b) - F(a), where F is any antiderivative of f such that F'(x) = f(x).

Chapter 2: Integration by Parts
The integration by parts formula is ∫ u dv = uv - ∫ v du.
This technique is derived directly from the product rule of differentiation: d/dx(uv) = u'(x)v(x) + u(x)v'(x).
When selecting which term to set as u, follow the LIATE priority rule:
1. Logarithmic functions (e.g. ln(x))
2. Inverse trigonometric functions (e.g. arctan(x))
3. Algebraic functions (e.g. x^2, 3x)
4. Trigonometric functions (e.g. sin(x), cos(x))
5. Exponential functions (e.g. e^x, 2^x)

Chapter 3: Taylor and Maclaurin Series
A Taylor series represents a smooth function as an infinite sum of polynomial terms calculated from the values of its derivatives at a single point a:
f(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a) + (f''(a)/2!)(x - a)^2 + ... = ∑_{n=0}^{∞} (f^(n)(a) / n!) (x - a)^n
When a = 0, the series is called a Maclaurin series.
Essential Maclaurin expansions:
- e^x = 1 + x + x^2/2! + x^3/3! + ... (Radius of convergence R = ∞)
- sin(x) = x - x^3/3! + x^5/5! - ... (Radius of convergence R = ∞)
- cos(x) = 1 - x^2/2! + x^4/4! - ... (Radius of convergence R = ∞)
- 1/(1 - x) = 1 + x + x^2 + x^3 + ... (Radius of convergence |x| < 1)
